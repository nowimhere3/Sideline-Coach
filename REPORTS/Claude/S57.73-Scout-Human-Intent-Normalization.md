# SCOUT LEXICAL NORMALIZATION

`SCOUT_HEAD` accepts `scouts?` (case-insensitive), so `Scouts this play`, `SCOUTS THIS PLAY`, `scouts, investigate…` are the same destination as `Scout this play`. Singular and plural are one destination; nothing stores the plural. Mixed case (`sCoUtS tHiS pLaY`) is covered.

# SCOUT CARRIER / DIRECTIVE GRAMMAR

New closed shape (first meaningful line only, reusing the Slice 4 carrier vocabulary), object must be `[the] scout(s)`:
- `[please|kindly] send|route|give|hand|pass|dispatch|assign [this play|the play|this one|this|it] [off] to [the] Scout(s)`
- `I would like|I'd like|I want to send [this/it to] [the|some] Scout(s)`
- `Run [the] Scout(s) on|over|against this/it/this play`

Same tail rules as the existing directive: end of line, or `:` `,` `;` ` - `/` — `, or a trailing `.`/`!`; the rest of the Play becomes the Scout objective (`executionPrompt`). A carrier followed by anything else (e.g. `I would like to send some scouts reports to Claude`) is not a directive.

# SCOUT CONTROL-TITLE BEHAVIOR

`SCOUT_TITLE` is ALL CAPS and limited to a closed noun set: `SCOUT(S) RECON | RECONNAISSANCE | MISSION | SWEEP`, optionally followed by a dash objective or emphasis markers. `SCOUT REPORT SUMMARY`, `SCOUT RECON FINDINGS`, `Scout Recon`, `scout recon` stay prose. A plain-head failure no longer masks the title: the three shapes are tried in turn.
