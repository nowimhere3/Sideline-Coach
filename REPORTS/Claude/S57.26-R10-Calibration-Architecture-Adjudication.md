## 2. What S57.1 Actually Requires vs. What AGY Added

### 2.1 The Governing Architecture (S57.1 §12.3)
S57.1 §12.3 states the complete graduation requirement in four concise bullets:
> - $\ge 30$ resolved (non-pending, non-excluded) outcomes in the class across all targets
> - 3-bin reliability check: each bin's observed rate within $\pm 10$ pp of mean predicted
> - Brier score beats the base-rate predictor (the class's pooled first-pass rate)
> - recomputed weekly; a class that fails later *un-graduates* back to words

### 2.2 What AGY Added (Deconstruction)
1. **3 Equal-Width Intervals $[0, 1/3)$, $[1/3, 2/3)$, $[2/3, 1]$:** **VALID / REQUIRED.** Standard partition for 3-bin calibration diagrams with boundary handling $\min(2, \lfloor 3p \rfloor)$.
2. **`n >= 5` Threshold for Bin Evaluable:** **VALID DERIVATION.** Directly follows existing codebase constant `SHADOW_MIN_N.calibrationBin = 5` (`dev-views.ts:41`).
3. **Minimum 2 Evaluable Bins to Graduate:** **VALID SAFEGUARD.** Prevents a task class where all 30 reps cluster in a single bin from claiming multi-bin calibration.
4. **Ignoring Sparse Bins ($1 \le n < 5$):** **VALID STATISTICAL NECESSITY.** On small samples ($n \le 4$), binomial noise makes a 10pp tolerance mathematically impossible or volatile.
5. **Invented 25pp Sparse-Bin Exception:** **REJECTED.** AGY proposed that if a sparse bin has error $> 25$pp, it vetoes graduation. This was an ad-hoc invention without architectural backing.

## 3. Evaluation of Candidate Bin Policies

### Option A — AGY Rule (Evaluable $\ge 5$, Min 2 Bins, Sparse Veto at $> 25$pp)
- **Flaw:** Evaluating a 25pp threshold on $n=1$ or $n=2$ observations is statistically indefensible. If a bin has $n=1$ with predicted probability $p = 0.25$, an observed failure ($y=0$) produces error $|0.0 - 0.25| = 25$pp. A single observation of an expected failure would immediately trigger or flirt with the tripwire purely by random Bernoulli draw ($P(y=0) = 0.75$).
- **Verdict:** **REJECTED.** Introducing noisy tripwires on sub-sample noise undermines deterministic reliability.

### Option B — Only Evaluate Populated Bins ($n \ge 1$, All Populated Must Be Within $\pm 10$pp)
- **Flaw:** For $n=1$, the only possible observed rates are $0.0$ and $1.0$. Unless predicted probability is $\le 0.10$ or $\ge 0.90$, a single observation *guarantees* an error $> 10$pp. For $n=4$, observed rates are in discrete steps of $0.25$. A perfectly calibrated model with $p=0.50$ will see 1 success out of 4 with $25\%$ probability, generating an observed rate of $0.25$ and error of $25$pp.
- **Verdict:** **REJECTED.** Would permanently block or flap graduation due to quantization error on small bins.

### Option C — Supported-Bin Policy (Recommended)
- **Design:**
  - A bin is **evaluable** if and only if $n_b \ge \text{SHADOW\_MIN\_N.calibrationBin}$ ($5$).
  - Every evaluable bin must satisfy $|\bar{y}_b - \bar{p}_b| \le 0.10$.
  - To graduate, a task class must have total $N \ge 30$, beat the pooled base-rate Brier score, and have **at least 2 evaluable bins** that both pass the $\pm 10$pp check.
  - Sparse bins ($0 \le n_b < 5$) are reported in Dev Mode with their sample size and flagged as `sufficient: false`, but carry no veto power.
- **Verdict:** **ADOPTED.** It is statistically robust, clean, and directly aligns with S57.1 §12.3 and R7 code precedent.

## 4. Why Option C is the Right Balance for Sideline

### Plain English for Coach
In baseball, if a batter goes 0-for-1 in a single pinch-hit at-bat, you don't conclude he has a .000 batting average for the season. You wait until he has enough plate appearances to separate skill from random luck.

In Sideline, when our AI router predicts how likely a Player is to succeed, it splits plays into three buckets: Low chance ($0–33\%$), Medium chance ($34–66\%$), and High chance ($67–100\%$).
- If a bucket has only 1 or 2 plays in it, a single bad break swings the bucket's score by $50\%$ or $100\%$. Holding that bucket to a strict $\pm 10\%$ test is testing pure luck, not AI accuracy.
- Option C says: A bucket only counts when it has at least **5 real plays** in it.
- To prove the router isn't a one-trick pony, we require at least **2 buckets** with 5+ plays each, and both must hit within $10\%$ of reality.
- Meanwhile, the **Brier score** check makes sure our predictions are genuinely smarter than just guessing the league average.
- And if the AI ever gets sloppy or provider models drift, the weekly check automatically strips the badge and drops back to words.

### Technical Defense
1. **Separation of Calibration and Discrimination:** Calibration is checked via reliability bins; discrimination (sharpness) is checked via Brier skill score against the pooled base-rate predictor ($BS_{\text{model}} < BS_{\text{base}}$).
2. **Standard Error Bounds:** At $n=5$, standard error of proportion for $p=0.8$ is $\sqrt{0.8 \times 0.2 / 5} \approx 0.179$. At $n=10$, it is $0.126$. Demanding $\pm 0.10$ tolerance on $n < 5$ is demanding precision tighter than the binomial variance allows.
3. **No Deadlock:** In a well-functioning router, low-probability plays ($p < 0.33$) are rarely dispatched intentionally. Requiring all 3 bins to reach $n \ge 5$ would trap competent routers in perpetual shadow mode. Requiring $\ge 2$ evaluable bins ensures calibration across multiple regimes (e.g. medium and high confidence) without requiring deliberate self-sabotage.

## 5. Exact Deterministic Graduation Algorithm

For a task class $C \in \{\text{'architecture'}, \text{'implementation'}, \text{'quick'}, \text{'default'}\}$ evaluated at timestamp `now`:

1. **Eligible Observation Set:**
   Extract all Film plays $i$ where:
   - `decision.recommendation` carries frozen predicted probability $p_i$ for the chosen route.
   - `decision.profile.taskClass === C`.
   - `firstPass` resolution state is closed and skill-eligible (`clean`, `repaired`, or `failed` with cause `player-defect`). Pending and excluded outcomes are filtered out.
   - Let $N_C = \text{count of eligible plays}$.

2. **Gate 1 — Sample Size:**
   $$N_C \ge 30$$
   *(If false: status is `'shadow'`, graduation denied).*

3. **Gate 2 — Brier Skill Score:**
   $$\bar{y}_C = \frac{1}{N_C} \sum_{i=1}^{N_C} y_i, \quad y_i \in \{0, 1\}$$
   $$BS_{\text{model}} = \frac{1}{N_C} \sum_{i=1}^{N_C} (p_i - y_i)^2$$
   $$BS_{\text{base}} = \bar{y}_C (1 - \bar{y}_C)$$
   $$\text{Condition: } BS_{\text{model}} < BS_{\text{base}}$$
   *(If false: status is `'shadow'` or `'ungraduated'`, graduation denied).*

4. **Gate 3 — 3-Bin Reliability:**
   Partition $p_i \in [0, 1]$ into bins $b \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ using:
   $$b = \min(2, \lfloor 3 p_i \rfloor)$$
   For each bin $b$:
   - $n_b = \sum_{i \in b} 1$
   - $\bar{p}_b = \frac{1}{n_b} \sum_{i \in b} p_i$ (if $n_b > 0$, else `null`)
   - $\bar{y}_b = \frac{1}{n_b} \sum_{i \in b} y_i$ (if $n_b > 0$, else `null`)
   - $\text{evaluable}_b \iff n_b \ge 5$
   - $\text{error}_b = |\bar{y}_b - \bar{p}_b|$ (if $\text{evaluable}_b$, else `null`)
   - $\text{passes}_b \iff \text{evaluable}_b \implies \text{error}_b \le 0.10$

5. **Gate 4 — Multi-Bin Support:**
   $$\text{evaluableCount} = \sum_{b=0}^2 \mathbf{1}_{\{\text{evaluable}_b\}} \ge 2$$
   $$\text{reliabilityCriterionMet} \iff (\text{evaluableCount} \ge 2) \land (\forall b \text{ with } \text{evaluable}_b: \text{passes}_b)$$

6. **Graduation Verdict:**
   $$\text{graduated} \iff (N_C \ge 30) \land (BS_{\text{model}} < BS_{\text{base}}) \land \text{reliabilityCriterionMet}$$

7. **Reversibility (Un-Graduation):**
   If a previously graduated task class fails Gate 2 or Gate 3 during weekly recomputation, its state reverts immediately to `'ungraduated'`, revoking percentage display until all gates pass again.

## 6. Semantics of `calibratedFirstPass`

### Verdict: Model A (Authorization, Not Transformation)
- S57.1 §5.3 establishes that the Bayesian posterior mean `firstPass.mean` from Beta-Binomial partial pooling is already Sideline's optimal point estimate of first-pass acceptance.
- S57.1 §12.2 and §12.3 establish that "calibration graduation" is the empirical proof that this posterior mean matches real-world frequencies.
- **`calibratedFirstPass` is Model A:** Once a task class graduates, the system is authorized to expose `primaryOption.scores.firstPass.mean` directly to the Coach as a calibrated probability.
- There is **no Platt scaling, isotonic regression, or mathematical transformation** applied. The math stays pure, transparent, and grounded in the Beta posterior.

```typescript
// Inside recommendRoute()
const isGraduated = graduationStore.isGraduated(profile.taskClass);
const calibratedFirstPass = isGraduated ? primaryOption.scores.firstPass.mean : undefined;
```

## 7. Rounding and Precision Rules

To preserve architectural precision and avoid compounding rounding errors:

1. **Belief & Recommendation Math:**
   - Keep unrounded standard IEEE 754 floats ($0.0 \dots 1.0$) across all internal computations (`firstPass.mean`, Brier scores, bin deviations).
2. **`calibratedFirstPass` Field:**
   - Store unrounded (or float precision) in `RoutingRecommendation.strength.calibratedFirstPass`.
3. **Dev Mode Presentation:**
   - Format Brier scores to 4 decimal places (e.g. `0.0842`).
   - Format bin errors and rates to 1 decimal percentage point (e.g. `8.3%`).
4. **Dad Mode Presentation (`dad-advisory.ts`):**
   - Round to the nearest whole integer percentage strictly at copy generation:
     $$\text{percentStr} = \text{Math.round}(calibratedFirstPass \times 100) + \text{"\%"}$$
     $$\text{line} = \text{"Suggest " } + \text{who} + \text{" · " } + \text{percentStr}$$
   - Verified length budget: Maximum possible line length is 26 characters (e.g., `"Suggest Antigravity · 100%"`), which strictly obeys `DAD_LINE_MAX = 28`.
