# Options

**A. Player Control prerequisite slice, then R9 (recommended).** A small, adapter-certified Player Control Play before R9. It adds two things:
1. **Structured provider-limit interruption.**
   - The turn event gains an optional `blocker: { kind: 'provider-limit'; pool; window?; resetsAt? }`.
   - It is set **only** from provider-emitted structured evidence on that Play's own stream:
     - Codex: certify and allowlist the `turn/completed` error kind (`usageLimitExceeded` / `rateLimitExceeded`, with `resetsAt`), after a probe records the real wire shape.
     - Claude: the `rate_limit_event` inside the Play's own stream, correlated to that turn by the adapter.
   - It is never inferred from text. Adapters without such evidence never set it.
   - The Ledger records it next to the outcome.
2. **Session-identity precondition.**
   - Project a per-instance `sessionKey` in the roster's routing capability.
   - Let a dispatch carry `expectedSessionKey`, which the Stadium refuses on mismatch (`reason: 'session-changed'`).
   - R9 records the key at arm time. `reopenFresh` then turns a Scheduled continuation into `needs-attention`, never a fresh-context send.

   Then R9 is built exactly as mapped above.

**B. R9 with correlation heuristics.** Offer Schedule when a turn fails while the Player's pool is LOW/CRITICAL with a known reset, and skip the session precondition.
- Fastest.
- Violates "do not manufacture it from UI heuristics" and "same Player continuation must be honest".
- **Not recommended.**

**C. R9 infrastructure only, dormant.** Build the store, scheduler, revalidation and APIs, but show no Schedule affordance until Option A lands.
- Safe and testable.
- Ships no Dad-visible value, and the continuation contract still cannot be proven end-to-end.

**D. Narrow R9 V1 to one adapter** (Codex only, after certifying its error kind). Session identity still needs Option A item 2.
